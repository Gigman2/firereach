# Flame colour guide

**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** Approved, implementing
**Adds:** a tenth guide and a tenth subcategory

## Why

Nothing in the nine shipped guides says what the colour of a flame means. The
project owner asked for it after fire training, citing "a blue flame may
indicate presence of electricity."

That claim and this guide's central rule look contradictory but are not. They
describe two different situations, and keeping them apart is the whole design:

- **On an appliance you are using** (gas burner, kerosene stove, wick lamp) a
  crisp blue flame is *complete combustion*, and a lazy yellow or orange one is
  the warning: incomplete combustion producing carbon monoxide. This is the
  guide's subject.
- **At a fire you are deciding how to fight**, blue-white arcing at a socket,
  switch or cable means electricity is involved, so isolate the power and keep
  water off it. This is an extinguisher-selection cue and it is sound. It is
  what the training taught.

An earlier draft carried a "What a flame cannot tell you" section correcting
the training claim. It was wrong to correct it, because it was answering the
second situation with the first one's rule. The section is dropped. Its two
uncontested bullets survive, reframed as the situation they actually belong to.

### Body

Written to the micro-format `GuideBody` parses: prose lines, headings ending
in a colon, bullets opening with `- `. No em dashes, per the standing rule
pinned by `noEmDashes.test.ts`. No organisation names in the prose, per the
project owner's rule recorded in `safetyContent.ts`.

The opening paragraph states the appliance framing explicitly. Without it,
"blue is what you want" could be read as a rule about a fire, which is the
exact conflation this guide exists to avoid.

Two headings open with `If `, so `parseGuideBody` classifies them crisis and
`GuideBody` renders them in `theme.emergencyText`: **If the flame has
changed** and **If you are choosing how to put a fire out**. Both are read
while something is wrong, so the emphasis is correct. Both must be added to
`EXPECTED_CRISIS` in `guideBody.test.ts`, which is that pin working as
designed: new content has to come back through the list.

The closing line, "This guide is about what a flame looks like. It does not
cover gas cylinders, regulators, hoses, or storage", mirrors the scope
disclaimer already in `hazard-cooking` so the two do not appear to disagree
about whether gas is covered.

### Sources

Every source carries `unverified: true`. The prose was drafted from general
fire-safety knowledge, not read out of the cited documents, and this project
treats a citation nobody has checked as no citation at all.

Two mechanisms already handle that state and both are relied on here rather
than worked around:

- `badgeText` (`safetyContent.ts:200`) finds no verified source and returns
  "Sourced from published guidance · awaiting review", naming no publisher.
- `reviewPacket.mjs:93` marks each one "⚠ UNVERIFIED — please confirm or
  replace", and `build-review-packet.mjs` lists them all for the reviewer.

No `url` field is written. `contentValidate.mjs` deliberately does not require
one, on the stated grounds that demanding a URL invites a fabricated one.

### Open questions for the reviewer

Five, the load-bearing ones being that no citation has been checked, that
blue-is-clean needs confirming for locally made kerosene stoves and wick lamps
rather than only piped gas, and that the appliance-versus-fire distinction is
worded so it does not read as contradicting the owner's fire training.

## Out of scope

- Gas cylinders, regulators, hoses and storage stay uncovered, as they are today.
- No change to the nine existing items. Charcoal and generator carbon monoxide
  is repeated from `hazard-cooking` and `hazard-electrical` deliberately: a
  reader who opens this guide should not have to open two others to be safe.
- No positive restatement of "a blue flame may indicate electricity" beyond
  the arcing bullet. Offered to the owner and not taken up.
